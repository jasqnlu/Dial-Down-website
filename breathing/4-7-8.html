How to do 4-7-8 breathing

Breathe in for 4, hold for 7, breathe out for 8. A simple, slow breathing pattern for winding down and falling asleep. Here's how to do it, and what to do if the hold feels too long.

What is 4-7-8 breathing?

4-7-8 breathing is a slow breathing technique with three parts: a 4-count inhale through your nose, a 7-count breath hold, and a long 8-count exhale through your mouth. It comes from traditional yogic breathing (pranayama) and was popularized by Dr. Andrew Weil.

The key is the long exhale. It lasts twice as long as the inhale, and breathing out slowly is one of the most reliable ways to slow your breathing and help your body shift toward a calmer, resting state. That's why many people use 4-7-8 at bedtime or when they feel anxious or wound up.

PhaseHowCount
InhaleQuietly, through your nose4
HoldKeep the air in, stay relaxed7
ExhaleFully, through your mouth, with a soft “whoosh”8

How to do 4-7-8 breathing, step by step

  1. Sit with your back straight, or lie down if you're using it to fall asleep.
  2. Rest the tip of your tongue just behind your upper front teeth, and keep it there the whole time.
  3. Breathe out completely through your mouth.
  4. Close your mouth and breathe in quietly through your nose for a count of 4.
  5. Hold your breath for a count of 7.
  6. Breathe out completely through your mouth for a count of 8.
  7. That's one cycle. Repeat 3 more times, for 4 cycles in total.

Four cycles takes about a minute and a quarter. The counts don't have to be exact seconds. What matters is keeping the 4 : 7 : 8 ratio.

If you can't hold for 7: the beginner version

Holding for 7 counts feels long at first, especially if you're stressed. Don't strain. Instead:

  • Count faster. Keep the same ratio but speed up your counting, so the whole cycle is shorter. As it gets easier, slow your counting down.
  • Try a gentler pattern first. Breathing in for 4 and out for 6, with no hold, gives you the long, calming exhale without the hold.
  • Stop if you feel lightheaded. Feeling a little lightheaded at first is common. Go back to normal breathing for a few breaths, then try again with faster counting.

Using 4-7-8 breathing to fall asleep

Do it once you're lying in bed with the lights off. Do 4 cycles, then let your breathing return to normal and notice how your body feels. If your mind is still racing, do another 4 cycles. Many people find it works better after a week or two of daily practice, as the pattern starts to feel automatic and familiar.

A tip: practice twice a day, for example once in the morning and once at night, rather than only when you can't sleep. A technique you already know is easier to reach for when you're stressed.

4-7-8 vs. box breathing vs. the physiological sigh

All three are slow, simple breathing patterns. They suit slightly different moments:

TechniquePatternGood for
4-7-8 breathingIn 4, hold 7, out 8Winding down, falling asleep
Box breathingIn 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4Staying calm and focused, e.g. before a presentation
Physiological sighTwo inhales through the nose, one long sigh outA quick reset in a stressful moment, in 1–3 breaths

If 4-7-8 doesn't suit you, it's worth trying the others. The best breathing technique is the one you'll actually use.

Is 4-7-8 breathing safe?

For most people, yes. It's gentle, and you're in control the whole time. A few things to keep in mind:

  • Breathe normally again if you feel dizzy or lightheaded.
  • Don't practice it while driving or anywhere you need to stay alert, since it's designed to relax you.
  • If you're pregnant or have a heart, lung or blood pressure condition, ask your doctor before doing breathing exercises that involve holding your breath. The beginner version without a hold is a good alternative.
  • Breathing techniques can help you feel calmer, but they don't treat anxiety disorders, panic disorder or insomnia. If those are affecting your life, talk to a doctor or mental health professional.
