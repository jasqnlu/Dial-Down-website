7 neck and shoulder stretches for tension

A 7-minute routine you can do without leaving your chair. Good for stiff necks, tight shoulders and that hunched-over feeling after a day at a screen.

Neck and shoulder tension usually builds up from holding one position for a long time: leaning toward a screen, looking down at a phone, or carrying stress in your shoulders without noticing. Your head drifts forward, and the muscles at the back of your neck and the tops of your shoulders work overtime to hold it up.

This routine goes from gentle warm-ups to deeper stretches and finishes by opening the chest, which helps undo the rounded-shoulder posture. Neck stretches should always feel gentle. Move slowly, never force your head with your hands, and stop if you feel sharp pain, dizziness, or tingling in your arms. If your neck pain is new, severe or came from an injury, read when to see a doctor first.

Total: about 7 minutes. Stretches 1–6 are done sitting down. For #7 you'll need a doorway, so it's a good excuse to get up from your desk.

1. Shoulder rolls

A warm-up that gets blood moving through the tops of your shoulders before you stretch them.

  1. Sit or stand comfortably with your arms relaxed at your sides.
  2. Lift both shoulders up toward your ears.
  3. Roll them back and down in a slow circle, squeezing your shoulder blades together at the back.
  4. Do 5 backward rolls, then 5 forward rolls.

2. Chin tuck

The single most useful move for "tech neck". It pulls your head back over your shoulders and wakes up the small muscles at the front of your neck that help hold it there.

  1. Sit or stand tall with your shoulders relaxed.
  2. Gently draw your chin straight back, as if making a double chin.
  3. Keep your eyes level. Don't look down.
  4. Hold for 3–5 seconds, then release.
  5. Repeat 10 times.

Careful: Move gently and stop if you feel dizzy or have sharp pain.

3. Seated neck rotation

Restores your neck's turning range, which tends to shrink when you face one screen all day.

  1. Sit tall with your shoulders relaxed and level.
  2. Slowly turn your head to look over your right shoulder.
  3. Hold for 10–15 seconds, feeling a gentle stretch through your neck.
  4. Return to center, then turn to look over your left shoulder.
  5. Hold, then return to center.

4. Upper trapezius stretch

Stretches the muscle running from your neck to the top of your shoulder, the one that feels like a tight rope when you're stressed.

  1. Sit tall. Reach your left hand toward the floor, or hold the edge of your chair to anchor it.
  2. Gently tilt your right ear toward your right shoulder, keeping your left shoulder down.
  3. For more stretch, rest your right hand lightly on the left side of your head.
  4. Hold for 20–30 seconds, feeling the stretch along the left side of your neck and shoulder.
  5. Release slowly, then repeat on the other side.

Careful: Keep any pull light and slow. Skip this one if you have a neck injury, recent whiplash, or numbness in your arms.

5. Levator scapulae stretch

Targets the muscle behind the trapezius that connects your neck to the top corner of your shoulder blade. It's a common source of the "knot" at the base of the neck.

  1. Sit tall and turn your head about 45 degrees to the right.
  2. Tuck your chin slightly and look down toward your right armpit.
  3. Rest your right hand on top of your head and let its light weight deepen the stretch along the left side of your neck.
  4. Hold for 20–30 seconds without pulling.
  5. Release slowly, return to neutral, then repeat on the other side.

Careful: Use only the weight of your hand. Never yank your head. Avoid this stretch if you have a neck injury.

6. Seated upper-back cat-cow

Moves the upper back, which stiffens when you sit hunched. A stiff upper back makes your neck do more of the work.

  1. Sit tall on the edge of a chair with your hands resting on your knees.
  2. Inhale: arch your upper back, lift your chest and let your gaze drift up.
  3. Exhale: round your upper back, drop your chin toward your chest and draw your knees together.
  4. Flow slowly between the two, following your breath.
  5. Repeat 8–10 times.

7. Doorway chest opener

Hours of typing pull your shoulders forward and tighten your chest. This stretch opens the front of your body so your shoulders can settle back.

  1. Stand in a doorway and place your forearms on each side of the frame.
  2. Keep your elbows at shoulder height, bent at 90 degrees.
  3. Step one foot forward and gently lean through the doorway.
  4. Feel the stretch across your chest and the front of both shoulders.
  5. Hold for 20–30 seconds, then step back to release.

Careful: Don't push into shoulder pain. Skip it if you have a shoulder injury or an unstable shoulder.

How often should you stretch your neck and shoulders?

Little and often works best for desk tension. Try the full routine once a day, and do one or two of the first five stretches every hour or so on long screen days. Shoulder rolls and chin tucks take under a minute and are easy to do between meetings.

Your setup matters too. Raising your screen to eye level and keeping your elbows close to your body when you type reduces how much tension builds up in the first place.

Tension in your neck and shoulders often comes with stress. Pairing a stretch with slow breathing, like 4-7-8 breathing, can help you let go of both.

When to see a doctor

These stretches are for everyday stiffness and tension. They aren't a treatment for injuries or medical conditions. Talk to a doctor or physical therapist before stretching, and get medical help promptly, if your neck pain:

  • started after a fall, car accident or other injury, including whiplash
  • comes with numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms or hands
  • comes with a fever and a stiff neck, a sudden severe headache, dizziness, vision changes or trouble speaking or swallowing (seek emergency care)
  • is severe, keeps getting worse, or wakes you up at night
  • hasn't improved after a few weeks

If you have a neck or spine condition, or have had surgery, ask your doctor which stretches are right for you.
