7 lower back stretches for tightness

A gentle 10-minute routine for a stiff, tight lower back. Six moves on the floor and one you can do at your desk, each with how long to hold it.

Most everyday lower back tightness comes from sitting a lot, standing for long stretches or moving less than usual, rather than from injury. These stretches gently move your spine in each direction it can go (bending forward, arching back and twisting), and they loosen the hips and glutes, which pull on your lower back when they're tight.

Move slowly and breathe. You should feel a gentle stretch, never sharp pain. If anything hurts sharply, stop. If your back pain is new, severe or comes with other symptoms, read when to see a doctor first.

Total: about 10 minutes. The order keeps you on the floor, moving from lying on your back, to your front, to hands and knees, so you aren't getting up and down. If you only have a couple of minutes at work, do just #7.

1. Pelvic tilt

A small, safe first move that wakes up your deep core muscles and gently mobilizes the lowest part of your spine.

  1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor.
  2. Flatten your lower back into the floor by gently tilting your pelvis and engaging your abs.
  3. Hold the flattened position for 5 seconds.
  4. Release back to a neutral arch.
  5. Repeat 10–12 times, moving slowly with your breath.

2. Single knee-to-chest

Gently rounds the lower back and stretches the glute on the side you're hugging.

  1. Lie on your back with both knees bent, feet flat on the floor.
  2. Draw one knee up toward your chest and clasp your hands behind the thigh or shin.
  3. Keep the other foot flat and your lower back relaxed on the floor.
  4. Hold for 20–30 seconds, breathing evenly.
  5. Release and switch legs.

Tip: Holding behind the thigh rather than over the knee is easier on your knee joint.

3. Lying spinal twist

Also called windshield wipers. Twisting while lying down lets gravity do the work, so it's gentler than most standing or seated twists.

  1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat, arms out to the sides in a T.
  2. Keeping both shoulders on the floor, let your knees fall gently to one side.
  3. Turn your head to look the opposite way for a deeper stretch.
  4. Hold for 20–30 seconds, breathing into your low back and side body.
  5. Bring your knees back to center, then repeat on the other side.

4. Sphinx pose

A mild backbend that balances out all the forward rounding from sitting. It's a gentler alternative to cobra pose.

  1. Lie face down and prop yourself up on your forearms, elbows under your shoulders.
  2. Press your forearms into the floor and lift your chest, keeping your hips down.
  3. Let your lower back lengthen rather than crunching.
  4. Hold for 30 seconds, breathing steadily.
  5. Lower down slowly to release.

Careful: Stop if you feel sharp lower back pain. Arching isn't comfortable for everyone.

5. Cat-cow

Moves your whole spine, one segment at a time, in time with your breath. It's one of the best stretches for morning stiffness.

  1. Start on hands and knees with wrists under shoulders and knees under hips.
  2. Inhale: drop your belly and lift your chest and tailbone (cow).
  3. Exhale: round your spine toward the ceiling, tucking your chin and pelvis (cat).
  4. Flow slowly between the two, following your breath.
  5. Repeat 8–10 times.

6. Child's pose

A restful stretch that lengthens your lower back and the sides of your back (lats). It's a good way to finish the floor part of the routine.

  1. Kneel with your big toes touching and knees wide.
  2. Sit back on your heels and lower your torso forward between your knees.
  3. Stretch your arms overhead on the floor, palms down.
  4. Rest your forehead on the floor and let your lower back lengthen.
  5. Hold and breathe deeply for 60–90 seconds.

Careful: Skip it if kneeling hurts your knees. A pillow between your heels and seat helps.

7. Seated spinal twist (desk-friendly)

The one you can do in an office chair, no floor needed. It's good for breaking up long stretches of sitting.

  1. Sit cross-legged, or on the edge of a chair, with your spine tall.
  2. To twist right, place your right hand behind you and your left hand on your right knee.
  3. Inhale to lengthen your spine, then exhale and gently twist to the right.
  4. Hold for 20 seconds, keeping both sit bones on the seat.
  5. Unwind slowly back to center, then twist to the left.

How often should you stretch your lower back?

Short and often beats long and rare. Doing this routine once a day, or just the seated twist every hour or two on long desk days, is usually more helpful than one long session a week. Many people find mornings and evenings work best: mornings to ease stiffness after sleep, evenings to unwind after a day of sitting.

Slow breathing helps too. Breathing out slowly while you hold a stretch helps your muscles relax into it. Try breathing in for 4 counts and out for 6.

When to see a doctor

Stretching is for everyday tightness and stiffness. It isn't a treatment for injuries or medical conditions. Talk to a doctor or physical therapist before stretching, and get medical help promptly, if your back pain:

  • started after a fall, accident or injury
  • comes with numbness, tingling or weakness in your legs
  • comes with loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness around your groin or inner thighs (seek emergency care)
  • comes with fever or unexplained weight loss
  • is severe, keeps getting worse, or wakes you up at night
  • hasn't improved after a few weeks

If you're pregnant, recovering from surgery or have a spine condition, ask your doctor which stretches are right for you.

This guide is general information, not medical advice. It doesn't replace a diagnosis or treatment from a qualified professional. Stop any stretch that causes sharp pain.
